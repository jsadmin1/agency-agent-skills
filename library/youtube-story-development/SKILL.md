# YouTube Story Development

Create a reviewable story package. Do not render media, change a channel, upload, schedule, or publish.

## Authority and preflight

1. Resolve the operator-supplied project root, channel, work-order ID, assigned episode/output folder, and current state.
2. Read the workspace and project source-of-truth files before stale episode notes. If the operator supplies a studio profile (project, channel and episode roots, role ownership, tracker paths), read it and treat live trackers as newer than it.
3. Confirm the assignment authorizes story development. Treat missing channel identity, missing work order, ambiguous output ownership, or an open fatal gate as `STOP`.
4. Work only in the assigned episode or artifact path. Do not edit the canonical queue, a launched channel bible, another agent's folder, shared renderers, or publishing state.
5. Copy [assets/story-work-order.md](assets/story-work-order.md) into the assigned artifact when no equivalent work order exists.

## Select the editorial mode

- **Original fiction:** build suspense from causality, character agency, constrained options, and an irreversible choice. Mark technical details as verified, deliberately fictionalized, or removed.
- **Factual documentary:** lock one viewer question and one bounded thesis. Create a claim/source matrix, chronology, uncertainty ledger, and item-level rights plan before polished narration. Prefer primary sources.
- **Source-faithful or religious:** identify the exact work, passage, translation, copyright/public-domain status, and interpretive boundary. Keep quoted source, historical context, later tradition, dramatization, and channel commentary visibly distinct.

Never use one mode's shortcuts in another. Do not invent dialogue, telemetry, quotations, motives, or countdowns for factual work. Do not present fiction or synthetic reconstruction as evidence.

## Workflow

1. **Lock the audience promise.** State the target viewer, episode-specific question, promised payoff, and why the topic belongs on this channel.
2. **Build exclusions.** Compare the prior three releases and active candidates. List repeated hook syntax, structure, setting, visual device, character dynamic, conclusion, and thumbnail grammar that this episode cannot reuse.
3. **Research before prose.** Resolve central claims and rights before writing factual/source-based work. Record exact source, locator, access date, confidence, allowed wording, uncertainty, and asset rights.
4. **Generate alternatives.** Produce at least three premises or thesis angles and at least ten hooks. Score hooks for clarity, consequence, curiosity, speakability, honesty, and payoff fidelity.
5. **Engineer the middle.** For fiction, map causes, choices, reversals, clock changes, and landed costs. For factual work, map claim, evidence, constraint, decision, result, and uncertainty. Every beat must change the viewer's understanding or available options.
6. **Draft from the structure.** Write the candidate script and timed storyboard. Keep evidence labels, source cards, diagrams, reconstruction labels, and sound intent in the storyboard rather than improvising them during editing.
7. **Write standalone Shorts.** Each Short needs its own hook, context, turn, payoff, and exact matching long-form relationship. Do not make chronological fragments or empty `Part 1` cuts.
8. **Red-team independently.** Use a different agent or clean review pass. Check causality/claim support, character or attribution, suspense/clarity, originality, advertiser suitability, rights, and channel fit. Never let the author self-promote the state.
9. **Return to the director.** Submit the package and [assets/story-gate-report.md](assets/story-gate-report.md) to whoever the operator designates as holding release authority. Only that director may mark `SCRIPT_LOCKED` or advance production.

## Hard stops

- No material claim without a source and allowed wording in factual work.
- No copyrighted translation, archival asset, music, footage, voice, or image without a recorded commercial-use basis.
- No real-person voice imitation, defamatory inference, graphic suffering as a hook, operational wrongdoing tutorial, or present-day tragedy farming.
- No interchangeable AI template. Each episode needs a unique thesis plus at least one episode-specific device such as a map, schematic, transcript, decision tree, dossier, source comparison, or timeline.
- If honest evidence cannot sustain the promised runtime, shorten or kill the premise. Never pad with unrelated history or invented detail.

## Completion receipt

Report the work-order ID, channel, editorial mode, title, promise, selected hook score, runtime/word estimate, output paths, source/rights status, originality exclusions, red-team decision, open risks, and requested next state. End with: `No media or YouTube state was changed.`
